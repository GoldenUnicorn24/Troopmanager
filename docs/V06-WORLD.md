# v0.6 – Living World

The campaign map is backed by serializable `WorldState`. Fifteen stable places, twenty-one roads, three original rival realms (Aschenbund, Hainpakt, Kupferliga), a neutral league, and three named rival commanders bootstrap from the validated versioned local catalog `app/src/main/resources/content/world-v1.json`. Existing region IDs (`keep`, `village`, `forest`, `mine`, `ruin`, `orc`, `monsters`) remain unchanged. Existing extra territories become stable map places connected to the home keep.

Player field armies and mission armies reference the existing aggregated player army pools. Dispatch reserves troops without removing or copying them into another pool. `GameState.away` adds standalone field reservations and mission reservations once. An engaged field army releases its reservation exclusively to the active battle; reconciliation returns survivors to its map location. Invasions redirect an existing hostile banner and retain its ID through siege resolution. Older invasions acquire a world overlay while their original fields remain untouched during migration; their previously committed provisions are grandfathered.

Marches traverse road legs over daily ticks. Terrain, road quality, force size, commander skill, morale, four seasons, persistent regional weather and chosen march policy determine progress. Food is prepaid into field stores and consumed each day; winter raises consumption. Depots consume real construction resources and hold finite food. Convoys follow roads and are lost when an unescorted hostile force occupies their next stop. Hunger and forced marches reduce the same troop pools and civilian population. Returning troops do not create soldiers.

Each faction keeps its own explored places, current sight and dated army observations. Enemy UI markers and strength cards use observations, including approximate ranges and stale report dates. Scouting costs resources and requires a reachable explored frontier. Towers, scouting missions, allies and espionage improve reports through shared state. The strategic AI uses its own dated observations and explored frontier. Recruiting and building spend its treasury; provisioning consumes its own food stock. Recruit orders charge the actual UnitType gold and iron costs upfront, reserve finite faction population, and complete after the same unit's trainingDays (no instant units). The target army remains at its capital while training. Iron is a visible finite treasury; mines produce 15 iron per day and existing workshops produce 3 per building. Destroyed target armies or lost training capitals cancel their paid queue without creating soldiers. Territory expansion, retreats and known threat pursuit follow explicit thresholds. Arrival estimates use remaining distance on the current road rather than restarting the full leg every day. Routing selects an accessible detour around peaceful borders; lost access replans an invasion or cancels it with a chronicle explanation. Weather or morale delays revise the forecast with the specific cause and preserve at least the advertised preparation window. Treaty and political decisions are owned by `DiplomacyEngine`; access restrictions apply to field armies and missions.

Regional missions now travel to the target, offer an intermediate route decision, perform their operation, and physically return before their surviving troops become available. Route choice changes march policy and outcome risk. Buying local provisions transfers finite stock from the local faction. The original targetless border missions retain their earlier duration for compatibility. Commander mission outcomes feed the character service record.

The story director produces state-dependent supply crises and a persistent two-stage border-signal chain. Choices spend resources or impose public-order/threat consequences, and resolved choices remain saved. Rival commanders gain victories, defeats, experience, injuries, epithets, rank and revenge motives. Experience affects combat/march capability; low ruler loyalty harms morale and can cause march refusal.

Weather and seasons affect travel and combat; seasonal production is handled by the economy engine. Campaign battles pass the real enemy roster, faction/army identity, weather, morale and terrain to the expanded battle engine. Geography and armies remain aggregated rather than per-soldier lists.

The Compose map supplies accessible region controls, heraldic army markers, scout/dispatch/redirect/recall/intercept actions, supply actions, mission decisions and story choices. Unexplored places withhold names, ownership, terrain and weather. Detailed cost and arrival forecasts are explained as estimates.

Validation rejects broken IDs, unknown references, duplicate reservations, invalid troop/resource quantities and incoherent mission overlays. `WorldEngineTest`, `V06WorldTest` and `WorldContentCatalogTest` cover migration, conservation, travel/return, hunger, four seasons, weather persistence, fair fog, finite logistics, story consequences, AI activity, field battle identity and invalid content. Integrated Gradle/build/lint evidence is recorded by the root implementation report; this subsystem's author did not run detached builds.

Current deliberate limits: graph-based strategic movement uses one place per completed leg; army drawing interpolates within that leg. Political ruler succession, diplomacy and society are separate integrated domains. Roads and places are data-driven now; the rest of the catalog categories retain typed Kotlin definitions. Historical observation expiration bounds save size, so obsolete reports are eventually forgotten. Campaign chronicle retention is 2,000 entries, consistent with the integrated Chronicle 2.0 screen.
